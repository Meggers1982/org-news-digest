**Federal Medicaid Spending on State Directed Payments Reaches $93 Billion Annually; New Limits Imminent**
Published: 2026-06-15 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/federal-medicaid-spending-through-state-directed-payments-nears-100-billion-annually-across-41-states-with-new-limits-set-to-reduce-funding-to-states/)

KFF estimates that 40 states and DC currently receive $93 billion annually in federal Medicaid spending through state directed payments (SDPs), with California ($10.6B), Texas ($6.3B), North Carolina ($5.2B), and Illinois ($5.1B) as top recipients. These payments face significant risk from forthcoming limits enacted in the 2025 reconciliation law, which reduced federal Medicaid spending by an estimated $911 billion through 2034.

**Flag:** 🔴 Action required
States relying heavily on SDPs for hospital and nursing facility reimbursement will need to restructure Medicaid financing; LTC providers in affected states should monitor implementation timelines and assess revenue exposure.

- **Medicaid SDP restructuring is the dominant story**: Three KFF releases detail the $93B federal exposure to state directed payments and the imminent limits from the 2025 reconciliation law. States with heavy SDP reliance—particularly for hospital and nursing facility payments—face significant financing challenges.

- **Medicare market dynamics shifting**: Analysis of MA rebates disadvantaging stand-alone Part D plans suggests accelerating consolidation pressure; combined with Part D enrollment/premium data, indicates ongoing structural evolution in how beneficiaries access drug coverage.

- **Coverage erosion signals**: First increase in uninsured population since 2019 marks a notable trend break that may have downstream effects on care transitions and coverage continuity for older adults approaching Medicare eligibility.

- **Federal policy tracking infrastructure expanding**: KFF launched or updated multiple trackers (mental health/SUD policy actions, USAID dissolution, health information trust) reflecting the volume of policy change requiring systematic monitoring.
