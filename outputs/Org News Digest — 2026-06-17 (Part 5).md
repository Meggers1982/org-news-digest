## KFF (Kaiser Family Foundation)
*Health Policy & Cost Research*

**New polling data on public trust in health information, social media, and AI use**
Published: 2026-06-17 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-polling-on-health-information-and-trust/) | [Related tracking poll](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-tracking-poll-on-health-information-and-trust-use-of-social-media-and-ai-for-health-information-and-advice/)

KFF released an interactive dashboard and accompanying tracking poll on health information trust. Key finding: approximately 30% of adults use social media for health information at least monthly, with slim majorities confident they can distinguish accurate information. Relatively few users verify the information they receive. The dashboard tracks exposure to and belief in false health claims, trusted sources, and vaccine attitudes.

**Flag:** 🟢 Note only
Relevant for understanding how older adults and caregivers may be receiving health information outside clinical settings; no direct policy impact but useful context for health literacy and misinformation discussions in LTC populations.

**2024 uninsured rate increased for first time since 2019**
Published: 2026-06-16 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/uninsured/key-facts-about-the-uninsured-population/)

KFF analysis of American Community Survey data shows the number and share of uninsured individuals grew in 2024—the first increase since 2019. The brief examines demographic characteristics of the uninsured population and summarizes access and financial implications of coverage gaps.

**Flag:** 🟡 Monitor closely
Coverage erosion affects aging populations' access to care and could signal pressure on Medicaid enrollment or workforce availability in home- and community-based services. Watch for state-level breakdowns and any connection to Medicaid unwinding trends.

**State-by-state tracker on immigrant access to services and enforcement activity**
Published: 2026-06-16 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/racial-equity-and-health-policy/recent-state-actions-related-to-immigrants-access-to-services-and-immigration-enforcement/)

Brief summarizes recent and proposed 2025–2026 state legislative actions affecting immigrant access to state-funded health coverage and other services, alongside immigration enforcement developments.

**Flag:** 🟡 Monitor closely
Direct implications for LTC workforce, which relies heavily on immigrant workers in direct care roles. States restricting access to services or increasing enforcement activity may see workforce supply effects.

## RAND Corporation
*Health Policy & Cost Research*

**EU policy mapping on family support and demographic challenges**
Published: 2026-06-16 | Source: RAND Research | [Link](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP71369.html)

RAND external publication examines how EU-27 member states support large families and single parents through cash benefits, tax measures, and in-kind schemes. The analysis maps policy approaches designed to address demographic challenges and child poverty across the EU.

**Flag:** 🟢 Note only
Indirect relevance—comparative framework for understanding how aging societies structure family support policies. May inform longer-term thinking on caregiver support or intergenerational policy design in U.S. context.

---

**Collaborative care effectiveness varies by clinical complexity**
Published: 2026-06-17 | Source: RAND Research | [Link](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP71340.html)

Study using latent class analysis examined heterogeneity in baseline clinical complexity among CLARO trial participants and assessed whether collaborative care versus enhanced usual care effects differed across complexity groups.

**Flag:** 🟢 Note only
Methodological interest for care model design; relevant if considering how integrated behavioral health models perform across patient acuity levels in older adult populations.

---

**Maternal care and infant health outcomes after noneconomic damage cap repeals**
Published: 2026-06-16 | Source: RAND Research | [Link](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP71366.html)

Analysis of CDC natality data covering 20+ million births across 18 states (2005–2019) examined whether repealing state noneconomic damage caps was associated with changes in maternal care and infant health outcomes.

**Flag:** 🟢 Note only
Peripheral to LTC; relevant for broader healthcare liability policy discussions but no direct aging services impact.
