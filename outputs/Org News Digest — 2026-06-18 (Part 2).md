## KFF (Kaiser Family Foundation)
*Category: Health Policy & Cost Research*

**Medicaid Work Requirements Take Effect in 2027: New Tracker Shows Which States Will Cut Enrollment**
Published: 2026-06-18 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-tracker-overview/)

KFF launched an interactive tracker monitoring state-by-state implementation of Medicaid work requirements mandated under the 2025 budget reconciliation law, which take effect January 2027. The tracker includes data on current enrollment, renewal outcomes, and existing state policies—critical baseline data as states prepare to disenroll beneficiaries who don't meet new work documentation rules. Previous work requirement pilots (notably Arkansas in 2018) showed disproportionate coverage losses among older adults ages 50-64 who faced barriers documenting exemptions.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Older adults ages 50-64 on Medicaid are among those most likely to lose coverage under work requirements—many have health conditions that limit work but don't qualify for disability. Pitch to AARP or Next Avenue as a "what you need to know now" explainer with state-specific guidance on how to document exemptions before the January 2027 deadline.
- **Trade/B2B:** Nursing homes and home health agencies in states with high Medicaid census should prepare for potential payer-mix disruptions—pitch to McKnight's as a planning guide for operators.

**Federal Medicaid Spending on State Directed Payments Hits $93 Billion—New Caps Coming**
Published: 2026-06-15 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/federal-medicaid-spending-through-state-directed-payments-nears-100-billion-annually-across-41-states-with-new-limits-set-to-reduce-funding-to-states/)

KFF estimates that 40 states plus DC currently receive $93 billion annually in federal Medicaid funding through state directed payments (SDPs)—supplemental payments to hospitals and other providers. California leads at $10.6 billion, followed by Texas ($6.3B), North Carolina ($5.2B), and Illinois ($5.1B). The 2025 reconciliation law imposes new limits that will reduce this funding, potentially affecting hospital budgets and access to care in Medicaid-heavy markets.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** These payments often prop up safety-net hospitals that serve low-income seniors. Pitch to local news outlets in high-SDP states (CA, TX, NC, IL) as an explainer on what funding cuts could mean for hospital access in their community.
- **Trade/B2B:** Strong angle for Skilled Nursing News or Provider Magazine—nursing facilities in states that use SDPs to supplement Medicaid rates could see payment reductions. Include KFF's companion briefs on [forthcoming policy changes](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/forthcoming-policy-changes-to-medicaid-state-directed-payments/) and [state-level spending data](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/spending-on-medicaid-state-directed-payments-before-new-limits-take-effect/).

**3 in 10 Adults Use Social Media for Health Advice—Few Fact-Check What They See**
Published: 2026-06-17 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-tracking-poll-on-health-information-and-trust-use-of-social-media-and-ai-for-health-information-and-advice/)

New KFF polling finds about 30% of U.S. adults turn to social media for health information at least monthly, seeking community connection and immediate answers. Slim majorities of these users say they're confident they can identify accurate information, but relatively few take steps to verify what they find. A companion [interactive dashboard](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-polling-on-health-information-and-trust/) tracks trends in health misinformation exposure and trust in various sources.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Older adults and their adult children are increasingly encountering health advice on Facebook, YouTube, and now AI chatbots. Pitch to Consumer Reports or AARP as a "how to spot bad health advice online" service piece, with specific red flags and verification strategies.
- **Trade/B2B:** Omit—limited direct relevance to long-term care operators.

**Medicare Advantage's Hidden Edge: How Rebates Undercut Stand-Alone Drug Plans**
Published: 2026-06-11 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicare/how-medicare-advantage-rebates-disadvantage-medicares-stand-alone-drug-plan-market/)

KFF analysis shows Medicare Advantage plans can use their payment structure to subsidize Part D drug coverage, offering lower premiums or reduced cost-sharing that makes MA drug plans appear cheaper—or even free—compared to stand-alone prescription drug plans. This creates market instability for stand-alone PDPs, potentially reducing options for traditional Medicare beneficiaries who prefer to keep their original Medicare coverage.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** This is a "hidden math" personal finance story for NYT Well, AARP, or Kiplinger's Retirement Report: why that "$0 premium" Medicare Advantage drug plan isn't necessarily a better deal, and what traditional Medicare users need to know during open enrollment.
- **Trade/B2B:** Omit.
