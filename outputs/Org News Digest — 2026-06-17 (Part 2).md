**Federal Medicaid State Directed Payments Near $100B Annually; New Limits Will Reduce Funding**
Published: 2026-06-15 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/federal-medicaid-spending-through-state-directed-payments-nears-100-billion-annually-across-41-states-with-new-limits-set-to-reduce-funding-to-states/)

KFF estimates that 40 states plus DC currently receive $93 billion annually in federal Medicaid spending through state directed payments (SDPs). California leads at $10.6B, followed by Texas ($6.3B), North Carolina ($5.2B), and Illinois ($5.1B). New limits enacted in the 2025 reconciliation law are expected to reduce this funding stream.

**Flag:** 🔴 Action required
SDPs are a major funding mechanism for hospitals and nursing facilities in Medicaid managed care. Forthcoming caps will directly affect LTC provider reimbursement in most states—assess exposure by state.

## Today's Themes

- **Medicaid financing pressure building:** KFF's suite of SDP analyses quantifies substantial federal Medicaid spending flowing to states ($93B annually) that is now subject to caps under the 2025 reconciliation law. Nursing facilities and hospital-based LTC services are directly exposed. Multiple states face significant revenue reductions.

- **Medicare Advantage market dynamics:** Continued KFF attention to how MA payment structure undermines standalone Part D viability and shifts beneficiary enrollment patterns. Relevant to SNP strategy and benefit counseling.

- **Workforce and coverage access signals:** Both the uninsured rate increase and state-level immigrant access policies are worth tracking for indirect effects on LTC workforce availability and stability.

- **Behavioral health policy shift:** KFF's new tracker on federal mental health/SUD policy under the current administration signals a more enforcement-oriented posture, with potential implications for SNF behavioral health services and dual-eligible care.
