### KFF (Kaiser Family Foundation)
*Category: Health Policy & Cost Research*

**Immigrants Make Up Nearly 1 in 5 Long-Term Care Workers — New Data Shows Scope of Workforce Dependence**
Published: 2026-06-18 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link](https://www.kff.org/immigrant-health/the-role-of-immigrants-in-the-u-s-health-care-workforce/)

New KFF issue brief quantifies immigrants' outsize role in the U.S. health care workforce, with particular focus on direct long-term care and hospital settings. The data arrives amid ongoing immigration policy debates and persistent workforce shortages in nursing homes and home care agencies.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Who's caring for your aging parent? Pitch a personal finance/caregiving piece to AARP or Next Avenue examining how immigration enforcement or visa policy changes could directly affect the availability and cost of home health aides and nursing home staff. Frame around what families should know now about workforce instability.
- **Trade/B2B:** For McKnight's or Skilled Nursing News: a data-driven analysis of which states and facility types are most exposed to immigrant workforce dependency — and what operators are doing to hedge against policy risk.

**KFF Launches Comprehensive Tracker for 2025 Medicaid Work Requirements — State-by-State Data Now Available**
Published: 2026-06-18 | Source: KFF Publications | [Link (main tracker)](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-tracker-overview/)

KFF released a multi-part interactive tracker covering the Medicaid work requirements mandated by the 2025 budget reconciliation law, set to take effect January 2027. The tracker includes state-level enrollment data, renewal outcomes, current policies, 1115 waiver status, and outstanding implementation questions. Related resources cover the policy mechanics in detail.

*(Note: Items 2–6 are components of this single tracker release — consolidated here.)*

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Millions of older adults on Medicaid could lose coverage if they can't prove work or exemption status — including caregivers, people with chronic conditions, and those cycling in and out of part-time work. Pitch to NYT Well or Consumer Reports as an explainer: "What the New Medicaid Work Rules Mean for Adults 50–64" with a focus on exemption pathways and documentation burdens.
- **Trade/B2B:** For Provider Magazine or state association newsletters: how nursing homes and home health agencies should prepare for potential Medicaid disenrollment waves in 2027 — and what early state waiver filings signal about exemption generosity.

### RAND Corporation
*Category: Health Policy & Cost Research*

**RAND Projects State-by-State Medicaid Enrollment Losses and Budget Impacts Through 2034 Under Reconciliation Law**
Published: 2026-06-18 | Source: RAND Research | [Link](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4098-1-v2.html)

RAND researchers modeled the enrollment and fiscal effects of key Medicaid provisions in the July 2025 budget reconciliation law (referred to as the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act"). The analysis provides state-level estimates of coverage losses and budget changes through 2034 — offering the most granular projections to date on who loses coverage and where.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Pitch a regional or state-focused story to local news outlets, Next Avenue, or AARP state newsletters: "How Many People in [State] Could Lose Medicaid Under the New Law?" Use RAND's state data to localize the impact, especially for the 50–64 cohort not yet Medicare-eligible.
- **Trade/B2B:** For McKnight's or state provider associations: which states face the steepest Medicaid enrollment declines, and what does that mean for nursing home and home health reimbursement stability?

**RAND Reviews 20 Years of Language Access Policy in U.S. Health Care**
Published: 2026-06-18 | Source: RAND Research | [Link](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP71321.html)

A narrative review of peer-reviewed studies, federal regulations, and professional guidelines from 2004–2025 on language access and interpreter services in U.S. health care. The analysis is policy-oriented, examining how reforms have (or haven't) improved care for patients with limited English proficiency.

**📰 Story angles:**
- **Consumer:** Millions of older immigrants rely on family members to interpret at medical appointments — often adult children or grandchildren. Pitch to Next Avenue or a multicultural outlet: what are your rights to a professional interpreter, and why do so many hospitals still fail to provide one? Frame as a caregiving/advocacy piece.
- **Trade/B2B:** Marginal angle for trade — could supplement a larger piece on serving diverse aging populations in long-term care settings, but not a standalone pitch.

## Pitch-Worthy Themes This Period

- **Medicaid squeeze on near-retirees:** Both KFF's tracker and RAND's state projections feed the same story — the 2025 reconciliation law's Medicaid changes will hit adults 50–64 hardest, right before they age into Medicare. This is a major consumer finance and health access story for AARP, Next Avenue, or a NYT investigation.

- **Immigration policy meets caregiving crisis:** KFF's workforce data pairs naturally with any immigration news cycle. A trend piece on "who cares for America's elderly — and what happens if they're deported or denied visas" could land at a general-interest outlet or as a Consumer Reports investigation.

- **State-level variation as a news hook:** Both the KFF tracker and RAND model offer state-by-state data — ideal for pitching localized versions of national policy stories to regional outlets, AARP state editions, or local NPR affiliates.
